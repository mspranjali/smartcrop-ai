About SmartCrop AI

A college machine-learning project for intelligent crop recommendation.

🎯 Problem Statement

Smallholder farmers often lack access to soil-testing services or agronomic expertise, leading to sub-optimal crop choices for their local conditions. Poor crop–soil–climate alignment can result in lower yields, higher input costs, and increased environmental strain from unnecessary fertiliser and water use.

SmartCrop AI addresses this gap by applying machine learning to a labelled dataset of soil and climate observations, enabling quick, data-informed crop suggestions through a simple web interface.

⚙ Methodology

  1. Dataset: 2,200 labelled records with seven features (N, P, K, temperature, humidity, pH, rainfall) covering 22 crop classes.
  2. Preprocessing: Dataset validated for null values and class balance; labels encoded with LabelEncoder.
  3. Train/Test Split: 80/20 stratified split to ensure each class is proportionally represented in both subsets.
  4. Model Training: Three classifiers trained and compared — Decision Tree, K-Nearest Neighbours, and Random Forest.
  5. Evaluation: Models evaluated on accuracy, weighted precision, recall, and F1-score on the held-out test set.
  6. Model Selection: The classifier with the highest weighted F1-score is saved and used for all web predictions.
  7. Deployment: Saved model loaded once at application startup; predictions served in real-time via the Flask web interface.

🌍 SDG Connection

SDG 2 – Zero Hunger

UN SDG 2 aims to end hunger and promote sustainable agriculture by 2030. Tools that help farmers make better-informed planting decisions could, over time, contribute to improved food security at the community level. SmartCrop AI demonstrates how machine learning can make agronomic knowledge more accessible, particularly in resource-constrained settings.

SDG 12 – Responsible Consumption & Production

SDG 12 calls for sustainable management of natural resources and reduction of waste. By recommending crops that are naturally aligned with local soil and climate conditions, this system illustrates how technology could support more resource-efficient farming — reducing the need for excessive irrigation, fertilisation, or pesticide application.

⚠ Limitations

  • The model is trained on a single public dataset; regional soil variation is not captured.
  • No temporal data — seasonal weather variability, El Niño cycles, etc. are not modelled.
  • Market prices, labour costs, and infrastructure are outside the model scope.
  • The system cannot account for pest pressure, diseases, or soil microbiome dynamics.
  • Predictions should be treated as a starting point for discussion, not as definitive advice.
  • Input values outside the training data distribution may produce unreliable outputs.
